# The Power Room: treatment

**Style:** Paper Pop-up Book · **Length:** 60 s · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · English narration + burned-in subtitles + `.srt`

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The journey of a watt.** A paper "spark" character travels from pylon to server across several spreads. Clear, but a cute traveller turns a reliability story into an adventure, and it takes too many spreads to tell in 60 s.
2. **Old way / new way.** Left page: a room wired without switchgear (one fault blacks out everything). Right page: the same room with it. A neat comparison, but half the film would explain something nobody builds.
3. **One lineup, pulled again and again.** Two spreads: *the path* (grid → transformer → switchgear → racks), then *inside the lineup*. Every job the switchgear does is a pull-tab: close the main, share out the feeders, trip on a fault, transfer to the generator. Each pull is one step of how it works.

**Chosen: 3.** Switchgear really is a box of mechanisms, and a pull-tab mechanism is the native move of a pop-up book. Every concept becomes a physical paper action: a breaker handle snaps, a copper strip slides, a source slider moves from UTILITY to GENERATOR. The turn (fault, then outage) is the moment the tabs pull themselves.

**Opening:** *a mechanism alone*. A locked macro on one paper breaker handle snapping ON before we see anything else.
**Ending:** *one piece stays up*. Every pop-up folds flat except the switchgear lineup; the camera cranes up and leaves the book small on the desk.
**Score shape:** a quiet pizzicato and music-box pulse that builds as power flows, cuts to silence at the outage, and returns brighter on the generator.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A pop-up book shows how a data center's low-voltage switchgear takes power from the grid, shares it out, isolates a fault, and swaps to the generator without the servers ever noticing.*

- **Setup:** power arrives; the switchgear distributes it (calm, regular pulses).
- **Turn:** a feeder faults (only its own breaker trips), then the grid goes dark (silence, colour drains to graphite).
- **Ending:** UPS bridges, generator starts, transfer, colour floods back. The book closes; the switchgear stands.

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only)

- **Macro "tiny world on a desk" photography plus engineering cutaway posters** (the cross-section plates in technical encyclopaedias). *Learn:* one idea per plate, labels that point, cutaway that shows the inside without losing the outside. *Don't take:* any drawing, layout or brand.
- **Robert Sabuda / Matthew Reinhart-style mechanisms.** *Learn:* back-to-front rises, pull-tabs as the event, layered V-folds. *Don't take:* any specific spread or character.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Narration subtitles: Fredoka, ~40 px, cream with a thick dark-brown outline, bottom centre, single English line.
- Paper signs (Lilita One) on cards with string: "LOW-VOLTAGE SWITCHGEAR"; small labels on the paper pieces (Fredoka): GRID, TRANSFORMER 400 V, UPS, COOLING, RACKS A, RACKS B, UTILITY, GENERATOR.
- One action word: "TRIP!" (italic display, double stroke) at the fault.
- No end card credit; the last frame is the book on the desk.

## 9. Technical accuracy notes

- LV distribution in data centers is typically 400 V (IEC) or 480 V (North America); the voice says "about four hundred volts".
- The main is shown as an air circuit breaker; feeders as breakers to UPS, cooling, and rack distribution.
- Selective coordination: the downstream breaker clears the fault, upstream breakers stay closed.
- Utility-to-generator transfer is shown as an open transition with an electrical/mechanical interlock (the two source breakers are never closed together). The UPS carries the IT load during the gap; the generator takes ~10 s to start.
