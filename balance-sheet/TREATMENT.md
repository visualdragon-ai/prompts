# How to Read a Balance Sheet: Treatment

Style: Sketchnote (one page of visual notes on dot-grid paper, drawn while you watch).
Language: English voice-over and subtitles. Length about 55 s. 1920×1080, 24 fps.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A question answered across a page.** A banner asks "What does it own, and who paid for it?" The page is filled in two columns: left, what a small business owns; right, who paid for it. An equals sign joins them. Then three numbered steps to read any balance sheet, and a pull-back to the finished page.
2. **Before/after split.** The left half of the page shows a bakery on opening day, the right half the same bakery a year later; the viewer learns the sheet by comparing the two. Clear, but it teaches change over time (closer to an income statement) before teaching how to read one sheet.
3. **One dollar's journey.** Follow a single coin from the bank into the till, into the oven, back out to the owner. Strong thread, but the balance sheet itself (a snapshot, two sides, one equation) would only appear at the end.

**Chosen: 1, with 3 woven in as the yellow thread.** A balance sheet is literally a page with two sides that must match, so the page-as-poster is the subject itself. One yellow coin carries the thread: it is borrowed from the bank (right side) and ends up as part of the oven (left side), showing that every dollar on the left came from somewhere on the right.

## 2. Logline and arc

A blank page asks what a business owns and who paid for it; as the pen fills in a small bakery's assets, debts and owners' share, one yellow dollar shows why the two sides always balance.

- **Setup**: the question banner; what's on the left (assets).
- **Turn**: the right side is not more "stuff" but *who paid*: liabilities and equity. The yellow coin crosses from the loan to the oven, and the totals meet at the equals sign.
- **Ending**: three numbered steps to read any balance sheet; pull back to the finished page, then push into the equation one last time.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Live graphic recording of a finance lecture**: learn the page plan (a title banner, two columns, one accent colour, numbered steps) and the rhythm of writing exactly what is said.
- **Good hand-drawn explainers of accounting** (the "it's just a scale" kind of teaching): learn to drop jargon, use one tiny example with round numbers, and state the equation only after the viewer has seen it work.
- **Don't take**: any layout, icons, lettering, characters or music from them. The bakery, its numbers and the icons are drawn for this film.

## 4. Page plan (the final poster, world coordinates 3000 × 1700)

```
                 [ banner: WHAT DOES IT OWN — AND WHO PAID FOR IT? ]          (top, centre)

   ASSETS  (what it owns)          =        LIABILITIES (what it owes)
   ┌───────────────┐                         ┌──────────────┐
   │ cash   $20k   │  (coin icon)            │ bank loan $50k│ (bank icon)
   │ flour  $10k   │  (sack icon)            │ unpaid bills $10k│
   │ oven   $70k   │  (oven icon)            └──────────────┘
   └───────────────┘                        +  EQUITY (what's left for owners) $40k
      total $100k                               total $100k
             (yellow fat arrow: one dollar, loan → oven)

   ① What does it own?   ② What does it owe?   ③ What's left?        (bottom row, numbered yellow banners)
```

Yellow: the coin and its path, the fat arrow, the equals sign's swash and the numbered banners. Everything else is black and grey.

Six different moves: whip, push, pan, ride the pen, shape match cut, pull back.

## 7. Cue map

Marimba and pizzicato at 92 bpm (0.652 s per beat), 4/4, D major, sparse.
- 0–2: silence, pen only.
- Bar 1 enters on the banner's first letter (J-cut under the whip).
- Left column: marimba ostinato, light pizzicato on each item landing.
- Right column: the same figure a third lower, bass pizzicato added.
- Breath before VO9: music drops out for about 1.2 s, pen only (silence #2). The first sound after it is a glockenspiel note on the coin.
- Equals sign: vibraphone chord on the downbeat.
- Three steps: kalimba accents on ①②③.
- Pull-back: harp roll and vibraphone closing chord; held to the end.
